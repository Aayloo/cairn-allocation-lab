## Final script

**[0:00–0:25] Opening**

Good afternoon. This is a five-minute investment case built around one question: can a simple trend overlay improve a diversified multi-asset portfolio? I will focus on design, results, underperformance, and the next decision.

**[0:25–0:55] Scope**

The L1 Multi-Asset Shadow Portfolio is a public-data research portfolio, not a live account. It contains no real money and no real profit and loss. My objective is not to claim significant alpha, but to turn an investment view into transparent weights, apply costs, record decisions, and discuss both good and bad results.

**[0:55–1:50] Portfolio design**

The strategic asset allocation, or SAA, uses eight liquid ETFs: forty percent equities, thirty-five percent fixed income and credit, ten percent gold, five percent commodities, and ten percent short-term Treasury bills.

The tactical overlay is simple. At each month-end, I calculate a ten-month trend signal for each equity sleeve. A positive signal keeps the sleeve at its strategic weight. A negative signal halves that sleeve and moves the proceeds to short-term bills.

The portfolio rebalances monthly. I charge ten basis points for each unit of one-way turnover, translate returns into Hong Kong dollars without hedging, and start the evaluation in June 2008. This is a risk-control rule, not a return forecast.

**[1:50–2:35] Results**

From June 2008 through October 2026, the tactical portfolio returned five point two zero percent a year. Annual volatility was seven point six four percent, the maximum drawdown was minus twenty-three point three two percent, and the Sharpe ratio was about zero point seven zero.

The static SAA returned five point eight six percent with volatility of eight point six eight percent. A 60/40 proxy returned eight point four three percent, with volatility of nine point six zero percent and a Sharpe ratio of about zero point eight nine.

So the answer is no. The trend overlay slightly reduced volatility, but it did not improve the maximum drawdown or create a convincing improvement in risk-adjusted return.

**[2:35–3:15] Why it underperformed**

The tactical portfolio lagged 60/40 by more than three percentage points a year.

Part of the explanation is the defensive design. Cash, gold, and commodities can reduce risk, but they also create an opportunity cost when equity returns are strong.

The trend rule has another weakness. It can reduce exposure after prices have fallen and re-enter after they have recovered. That may protect capital in a long downturn, but it can sacrifice upside in a fast recovery.

The result is evidence about the process, not proof of a superior strategy.

**[3:15–4:00] Current decision and risk**

At the October 2026 review, all three equity signals were positive, so the portfolio remained close to its strategic allocation.

The weights were approximately thirty-nine point four percent equities, thirty-five point seven percent fixed income and credit, nine point six percent gold, five point two percent commodities, and ten percent short-term bills.

The main risk is not visible from capital weights alone. Equities are forty percent of capital but contribute roughly sixty-one percent of model-based variance.

At the next month-end review, any negative ten-month equity signal will halve that sleeve and move the proceeds to bills. A material policy change requires a new frozen version and cannot be applied retrospectively.

**[4:00–4:35] Limitations**

This is a paper portfolio using adjusted-close data and US-listed ETF proxies. It includes a flat transaction-cost assumption, but not market impact, taxes, broker execution, or a live trading history. The results are unhedged HKD translations and do not establish real-money capability.

I do not claim statistical significance. The next research step is to test different start dates, add realistic constraints, and compare alternative risk-control methods.

**[4:35–5:00] Conclusion**

The value of this project is not a high-return claim. It is a reproducible decision process: define the policy, apply the signal, record the weights, charge the costs, measure the risk, and explain the result even when it is disappointing.

The model supports one decision: maintain or reduce equity risk at month-end through a documented process. Thank you. I am happy to take questions.
